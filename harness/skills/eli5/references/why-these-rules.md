# Why the eli5 rules are what they are

Sourced 2026-08-23, extended 2026-08-28, not invented:

- Writing to a five-year-old is the wrong target for adults — write to a smart
  newcomer instead ([Storytelling with Data](https://www.storytellingwithdata.com/blog/dont-explain-it-like-im-five)).
- Keep some jargon so the reader can search; only put "fun" visuals on writing
  that is genuinely clear ([Julia Evans](https://jvns.ca/blog/2020/11/15/simple-explanations-without-sounding-condescending/)).
- A single analogy reliably induces misconceptions; qualify it or add another
  (Spiro et al. 1989, *Multiple analogies for complex concepts*).
- People rate explanations HIGHER with more causal mechanism, and simplicity
  does not predict quality (Zemla et al. 2017, *Evaluating everyday
  explanations*) — which is why the budget is global, not per-section: quotas
  exist to kill filler, never mechanism or pacing. (Per-section quotas were
  dropped 2026-08-28 after a retrospective: they flattened every beat to the
  same weight and produced metric-chasing trims instead of better pages.)
- Depth calibrated to the listener's next action, and one level simpler than
  you estimate: practitioner consensus across audience-calibration guides
  (Wellspoken 2026 decision filter; the technical-explanation level matrix,
  MoltedOpus 2026 — overestimating depth loses the reader irrecoverably;
  underestimating merely invites "tell me more").
- Narrative transportation increases engagement and persuasion over exposition
  (Green & Brock 2000); narrative formats improve comprehension and interest
  for non-expert audiences (Dahlstrom 2014, PNAS); concrete stakes,
  unexpectedness and emotion make messages stick (Heath & Heath 2007, *Made
  to Stick*). None of this licenses hype: the no-exclamation,
  say-only-what-is-true rules above still bind.
- Curiosity is an information gap — opening one is what makes a reader need
  the next beat (Loewenstein 1994, *The psychology of curiosity*).
- The what-is / what-could-be contrast engine, audience-as-hero, and ending on
  the adopted world are Duarte's Sparkline, distilled from hundreds of
  persuasive talks ([HBR 2012](https://hbr.org/2012/10/structure-your-presentation-li), Duarte, *Resonate*).
- Opening on a single concrete data point before the abstraction, and letting
  the takeaway's complexity set the structure, is standing practice at
  [The Pudding](https://pudding.cool/process/how-to-make-dope-shit-part-3/).
- Words and pictures beat words alone (multimedia principle), but only when
  the channels complement rather than repeat each other (redundancy
  principle); segment content into pieces and offer details-on-demand instead
  of front-loading depth (Mayer's principles, surveyed in
  [Hohman et al., Distill 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles)).
- Plain language is not "dumbing down" — define the hard word, do not delete
  it ([NIH](https://www.nih.gov/institutes-nih/nih-office-director/office-communications-public-liaison/clear-communication/plain-language/presenting-your-information), ISO 24495-1:2023).
