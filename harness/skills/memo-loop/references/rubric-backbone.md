# Rubric backbone — the candidate-item pool

Five battle-tested frameworks, expanded into candidate binary items. **This is a pool to draw from, not
a template to apply** — a rubric that is all backbone has no teeth against this particular memo. Pick
the 5–9 axes the memo's genre actually needs, then write the memo-specific items that do the real work.

The rules every item must satisfy (binary, positively phrased, mid-length, substance-marker-anchored,
`[checkable]`/`[judgment]` tagged) live in `intake-and-rubric.md` Step 6 and are not repeated here.
Items below already conform; an item you adapt must still be checked against them.

If a promoted overlay is present, `<promotion_dir>/rubric-backbone.additions.md` loads **after** this
file — promoted axes can add to the pool, never replace it.

## BLUF

*Recommendation stated before reasoning; survives a reader who stops after paragraph one.*

- **[checkable]** The memo states its recommendation or bottom-line answer in the first paragraph,
  before any supporting reasoning.
- **[judgment]** A reader who stops after paragraph one already knows what decision is being asked for
  and what the author recommends.
- **[checkable]** The opening paragraph names at least one specific number, date, or quantity relevant
  to the recommendation — not just a general claim.

## Minto Pyramid / SCQA

*One governing thought at the top; supporting arguments grouped MECE, each independently provable;
SCQA narrative on-ramp.*

- **[judgment]** The memo has one clearly identifiable governing thought that every subsequent section
  supports.
- **[judgment]** Each supporting argument covers a distinct part of the case (mutually exclusive), and
  together they cover the whole case for the governing thought (collectively exhaustive).
- **[checkable]** Each supporting argument names at least one piece of evidence — a number, a cited
  source, or a named fact — that could independently prove or disprove it.
- **[judgment]** The memo's opening establishes the Situation, the Complication that disrupts it, and
  the Question the memo answers, in that order, before the Answer.

## Amazon 6-pager / PR-FAQ

*Self-contained (no outside context needed); outcome and stakeholder framing up front; objections
pre-empted; density — no padding.*

- **[judgment]** A reader with no prior context on this topic can follow the memo's argument without
  needing to ask a clarifying question first.
- **[checkable]** The memo states, in the first section, the specific outcome or stakeholder impact the
  recommendation produces.
- **[checkable]** The memo names at least one specific objection or counter-argument a skeptical reader
  would raise, and answers it directly.
- **[judgment]** Every sentence carries a fact, a number, or a claim that no earlier sentence already
  made — each one adds substance rather than restating it.

## Toulmin model

*Every claim has grounds; the warrant (the inferential leap) is explicit; rebuttal conditions
acknowledged; confidence qualified rather than falsely certain.*

- **[checkable]** Every load-bearing claim is followed by a stated ground — a fact, number, or source —
  rather than standing unsupported.
- **[judgment]** Where the memo infers a conclusion from its grounds, the inferential step — *why* the
  grounds support the claim — is stated, not left implicit.
- **[checkable]** The memo names at least one condition under which its recommendation would not hold,
  rather than presenting the recommendation as unconditionally correct.
- **[judgment]** The memo's stated confidence in each major claim matches the strength of the evidence
  offered for that claim — a weakly-grounded claim is stated with a qualifier that says so.

## Paul-Elder standards

*Eight reasoning-quality axes, usable nearly verbatim.*

- **[judgment] Clarity** — a reader unfamiliar with the topic can restate the memo's core argument in
  their own words after one read.
- **[checkable] Accuracy** — every number either recomputes from its stated source or is flagged as an
  estimate with a stated basis.
- **[checkable] Precision** — claims use specific figures or named entities rather than vague
  quantifiers ("many", "significant", "several") where a specific figure is available.
- **[judgment] Relevance** — every section bears directly on the stated decision, and each one supplies
  something the reader needs to make it.
- **[judgment] Depth** — the memo engages with the strongest version of the counter-case a
  well-informed opponent would actually make.
- **[judgment] Breadth** — the memo considers at least one alternative framing or option besides the
  recommended one, and states why it was not chosen.
- **[checkable] Logic** — each inferential step from grounds to conclusion is one the grounds actually
  license (a causal conclusion rests on causal evidence, not on a correlation offered in its place).
- **[checkable] Significance** — the memo states why this decision matters now — a named risk, cost, or
  deadline — not just that it matters.
