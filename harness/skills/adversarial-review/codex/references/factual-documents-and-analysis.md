# Factual Documents and Analysis Review

Apply these lenses to reports, research, factual documents, forecasts,
quantitative analyses, and evidence-backed prose.

## Claim-Evidence Alignment

- Identify the central claims and the evidence offered for each.
- Check whether citations support the exact proposition, population, period, and
  scope claimed.
- Distinguish direct evidence, interpretation, and speculation.
- Treat uncited background details proportionately; focus on claims material to
  the supported decision.

## Source Quality and Freshness

- Prefer primary, authoritative, and methodologically transparent sources.
- Check publication date, update date, jurisdiction, version, and applicability.
- Verify cited pages rather than relying on titles, snippets, or summaries.
- Look for retractions, corrections, superseding data, and meaningful conflicts.
- For legal, regulatory, standards, and policy claims, separate operative text
  from proposed, provisional, adopted-not-effective, guidance, commentary, and
  forecast material.

## Statistics and Quantitative Reasoning

- Check denominators, units, base rates, sample size, uncertainty, and time
  windows.
- Look for selection bias, survivorship bias, p-hacking, inappropriate averages,
  and misleading relative changes.
- Recalculate material quantities when inputs are available.
- Verify that charts, tables, and narrative describe the same values.

## Methodology and Reproducibility

- Check data provenance, inclusion rules, transformations, missing data,
  baselines, and sensitivity analysis.
- Determine whether another reviewer could reproduce the result.
- Treat hidden or unavailable methodology as uncertainty, not automatic proof of
  error.

## Causality and Generalization

- Challenge causal language based only on correlation or before-and-after
  comparison.
- Check confounders, controls, external validity, and population shifts.
- Verify that conclusions do not exceed the study design or data coverage.

## Counterevidence and Alternatives

- Search for credible evidence that would reverse or narrow the conclusion.
- Check whether alternative explanations were tested consistently.
- Do not demand exhaustive literature review unless the decision requires it.

## Internal Consistency

- Compare executive summary, body, footnotes, tables, and appendices.
- Check definitions, dates, totals, assumptions, and confidence language.
- Identify conclusions that contradict stated limitations.

## Audience and Decision Relevance

- Verify that the document answers the decision it claims to support.
- Check whether uncertainty, limitations, and material caveats are visible to the
  intended audience.
- Treat aesthetic preferences as subjective unless presentation causes a
  concrete misunderstanding or violates an explicit requirement.
- Treat acknowledged owner inputs and placeholders as questions unless they
  make the document's requested decision unsafe, impossible, or materially
  misleading.
